## Questions and explanations

### Question 1 [EASY]

**Craft and Structure / Words in Context**. ⏱ Target: 45 seconds

> Engineers in the Netherlands have spent centuries developing ways to manage water. Because about a quarter of the country lies below sea level, its system of dikes, pumps, and canals must be ______ maintained: even a small failure could allow seawater to flood farmland and cities.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?**

- A) rarely
- B) constantly
- C) casually
- D) temporarily

**Correct answer: B**

**Key explanation.** The text says that even a small failure could let seawater flood farmland and cities. Because this danger never goes away, the system must be maintained all the time. “Constantly” (continuously; all the time) fits this logic.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** “Rarely” means “not often.” Maintaining the system rarely would contradict the warning that even a small failure could be dangerous.
- **C:** “Casually” means “without much care or attention.” This conflicts with the serious risk the text describes.
- **D:** “Temporarily” means “for a short time only.” The danger of flooding is permanent, so maintenance cannot stop after a short time.

**Tip.** Look for the clue after the colon. A colon often introduces an explanation of the word in the blank.

### Question 2 [MEDIUM]

**Craft and Structure / Words in Context**. ⏱ Target: 60 seconds

> The following text is adapted from a short story.
> 
> Amara had expected the old market in Accra to feel chaotic. But as she walked between the stalls, she began to notice an underlying order: fabric sellers gathered in one lane, spice traders in another, and every vendor seemed to know exactly where each neighbor’s goods belonged. What had first appeared random was, in fact, highly ______.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?**

- A) systematic
- B) spontaneous
- C) ornamental
- D) provisional

**Correct answer: A**

**Key explanation.** The text contrasts a first impression (“chaotic,” “random”) with what Amara discovers: an “underlying order” in which each type of seller has a fixed place. “Systematic” means “arranged according to a clear plan or method,” which is the opposite of random and matches the examples.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **B:** “Spontaneous” means “happening without planning.” This describes something random, which is only what the market first appeared to be.
- **C:** “Ornamental” means “decorative.” The text is about how the market is arranged, not about how beautiful it is.
- **D:** “Provisional” means “temporary; arranged for now but likely to change.” Nothing suggests the arrangement is temporary; vendors know exactly where goods belong.

**Tip.** The phrase “in fact” signals a contrast with an earlier idea (“random”), so the correct word should mean the opposite of random.

### Question 3 [MEDIUM]

**Craft and Structure / Words in Context**. ⏱ Target: 60 seconds

> Conservationists in the Sahel, the semi-arid region along the southern edge of the Sahara, have planted large numbers of drought-resistant trees as part of the Great Green Wall initiative. The trees’ roots hold loose soil in place and help the ground absorb rainwater. Project leaders hope that, over time, the planting will arrest the spread of the desert into nearby farmland.

**As used in the text, what does the word “arrest” most nearly mean?**

- A) capture
- B) attract
- C) stop
- D) punish

**Correct answer: C**

**Key explanation.** Here, “arrest” means “to stop or slow the progress of something.” The trees hold soil in place and help the ground absorb water, so project leaders hope the planting will stop the desert from spreading into farmland.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** “Capture” reflects the everyday meaning of “arrest” (police taking a person into custody). A desert’s spread cannot be captured, so this meaning does not fit the context.
- **B:** “Attract” means “pull toward.” Project leaders want to prevent the desert’s spread, not draw it closer.
- **D:** “Punish” is linked to the legal meaning of “arrest,” but it makes no sense for a natural process such as the spread of a desert.

**Tip.** Common trap for international students: many English words have an everyday meaning and a less common academic meaning, and the SAT often tests the less common one. Put each answer choice into the sentence in place of the word and reread it.

### Question 4 [HARD]

**Craft and Structure / Words in Context**. ⏱ Target: 75 seconds

> Popular histories often credit a single inventor with each major breakthrough, but historian Tomás Ibarra argues that such accounts are ______. The printing press developed in fifteenth-century Europe, for example, depended on papermaking methods that had spread from China, ink recipes improved by many workshops, and metalworking skills shared across the continent. Crediting one person, Ibarra says, hides the collective effort involved.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?**

- A) exhaustive
- B) contentious
- C) ambiguous
- D) reductive

**Correct answer: D**

**Key explanation.** Ibarra says single-inventor stories “hide the collective effort involved,” and the text lists many contributors that such stories leave out. “Reductive” means “presenting something complex in a way that is too simple.” This precisely describes Ibarra’s criticism.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** “Exhaustive” means “complete and thorough.” Ibarra argues the opposite: the accounts leave out important contributors.
- **B:** “Contentious” means “likely to cause disagreement.” Ibarra disagrees with the accounts, but he criticizes them for oversimplifying, not for causing arguments.
- **C:** “Ambiguous” means “unclear; having more than one possible meaning.” The accounts are not unclear (they clearly name one inventor); Ibarra’s problem is that they are too simple.

**Tip.** When a text explains a criticism with examples, the correct word must name that exact criticism. Here the criticism is oversimplification.

### Question 5 [EASY]

**Craft and Structure / Text Structure and Purpose**. ⏱ Target: 60 seconds

> In the highlands of Ethiopia, farmers have grown teff, a grain with extremely small seeds, for thousands of years. About 150 teff seeds weigh the same as a single grain of wheat. <ins>Because of this, a farmer can carry enough seed to plant a large field in one small bag.</ins> Today, teff is also becoming popular in other countries, partly because it contains no gluten.

**Which choice best describes the function of the underlined sentence in the text as a whole?**

- A) It explains a practical advantage that results from a characteristic described in the previous sentence.
- B) It introduces a problem that the rest of the text attempts to solve.
- C) It presents evidence that challenges the claim made in the first sentence.
- D) It shifts the discussion from teff’s history to its popularity in other countries.

**Correct answer: A**

**Key explanation.** The previous sentence describes a characteristic of teff: its seeds are extremely small and light. The underlined sentence begins with “Because of this,” which shows that it describes a result. That result is a practical benefit: farmers can carry enough seed for a large field in a small bag.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **B:** The sentence describes a benefit, not a problem, and no later sentence offers a solution.
- **C:** The first sentence says teff has been grown in Ethiopia for thousands of years. The underlined sentence does not question this; it adds a practical detail.
- **D:** The shift to teff’s popularity in other countries happens in the final sentence, not in the underlined one.

**Tip.** Linking phrases such as “Because of this” reveal a sentence’s function. Here, the phrase signals cause and effect.

### Question 6 [MEDIUM]

**Craft and Structure / Text Structure and Purpose**. ⏱ Target: 75 seconds

> The following text is adapted from a short story.
> 
> Every morning, the smell of fresh bread drifted from Mr. Okafor’s bakery into the narrow street before the sun had fully risen. Neighbors joked that they could set their clocks by it. Children on their way to school slowed their steps outside his window, and even the stray cats seemed to gather there, waiting. When the bakery closed for a week in July, the street felt strangely unfinished, as if someone had removed a word from a familiar sentence.

**Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?**

- A) To describe the difficulties Mr. Okafor faces in running a small business
- B) To show how deeply the bakery had become part of the daily life of the street
- C) To explain why Mr. Okafor decided to close his bakery for a week
- D) To compare the habits of the neighborhood children with those of the stray cats

**Correct answer: B**

**Key explanation.** Each detail shows the bakery’s role in the street’s routine: neighbors “set their clocks by it,” children slow down, and cats gather. The final sentence confirms this. Without the bakery, the street feels “unfinished,” as if part of something familiar is missing.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** The text never mentions any business difficulties.
- **C:** The text says the bakery closed for a week but gives no reason for the closure.
- **D:** Children and cats are both mentioned as examples of the bakery’s effect on the street; the text does not compare their habits.

**Tip.** The last sentence of a literary passage often states or reinforces its purpose. Here, the comparison to “a word removed from a familiar sentence” shows how much the bakery belonged to the street.

### Question 7 [HARD]

**Craft and Structure / Cross-Text Connections**. ⏱ Target: 105 seconds

> **Text 1**
> Many city officials argue that building more roads is an effective way to reduce traffic congestion. By adding lanes to busy highways, cities increase the number of vehicles the roads can carry at one time. According to this view, traffic then flows more smoothly, and commuters spend less time traveling.
> 
> **Text 2**
> Transportation researcher Mei-Lin Chou examined traffic data from dozens of cities over two decades. She found strong evidence of what she calls “induced demand”: when new lanes were added, more people chose to drive, and the number of vehicles rose almost in proportion to the new space. Within a few years, congestion returned to its earlier level. Chou concludes that expanding roads rarely provides lasting relief from traffic.

**Based on the texts, how would Chou (Text 2) most likely respond to the officials’ argument in Text 1?**

- A) By agreeing that adding lanes reduces travel times permanently, as long as the lanes are built quickly
- B) By claiming that traffic congestion is a smaller problem in most cities than the officials believe
- C) By arguing that the officials’ proposal is too expensive for most cities to adopt
- D) By suggesting that the officials overlook how people change their driving habits when road space increases

**Correct answer: D**

**Key explanation.** The officials in Text 1 assume that the number of vehicles stays the same after new lanes are built, so more space means smoother traffic. Chou found that new lanes cause more people to drive (“induced demand”). The extra drivers fill the new space and bring congestion back. She would therefore say the officials ignore how drivers’ behavior changes.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** Chou concludes that expanding roads “rarely provides lasting relief,” which directly contradicts the idea of a permanent improvement.
- **B:** Chou does not question how serious congestion is; she questions whether new roads reduce it.
- **C:** Neither text discusses cost, so there is no basis for this response.

**Tip.** For cross-text questions, first restate each text’s main claim in a few words (Text 1: more lanes → less traffic. Text 2: more lanes → more drivers → same traffic). Then choose the answer that connects the two.

### Question 8 [EASY]

**Information and Ideas / Central Ideas and Details**. ⏱ Target: 60 seconds

> Mangrove forests grow along tropical coastlines in countries such as Indonesia, Bangladesh, and Brazil. Their dense, tangled roots slow incoming waves, which reduces storm damage and protects coastal villages from erosion. The roots also shelter young fish, many of which later become an important food source for local fishing communities. In recent decades, however, large areas of mangroves have been cleared to make room for shrimp farms and tourist resorts.

**Which choice best states the main idea of the text?**

- A) Mangrove forests provide important benefits to coastal communities, but many have been removed for other uses.
- B) Shrimp farms have become more profitable than traditional fishing in tropical countries.
- C) Mangrove roots are the most effective storm protection available to coastal villages.
- D) Tourist resorts in Indonesia, Bangladesh, and Brazil are the main cause of declining fish populations.

**Correct answer: A**

**Key explanation.** Most of the text describes the benefits of mangroves: storm protection, erosion control, and shelter for young fish. The last sentence adds that many mangroves have been cleared. Choice A combines both parts of the text.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **B:** The text never compares the profits of shrimp farms and fishing.
- **C:** The text says the roots reduce storm damage, but it never claims they are “the most effective” protection. This goes beyond the text.
- **D:** The text does not connect resorts to declining fish populations, and it does not identify any “main cause.”

**Tip.** Be careful with extreme words such as “most,” “only,” and “always.” A main-idea answer must be fully supported by the text.

### Question 9 [MEDIUM]

**Information and Ideas / Central Ideas and Details**. ⏱ Target: 60 seconds

> The following text is adapted from a short story.
> 
> Lucía had practiced the violin piece for months, yet when she stepped onto the stage of the concert hall in Montevideo, her hands felt as if they belonged to someone else. She looked out at the audience and found her grandmother in the third row, sitting perfectly still, hands folded in her lap exactly as they had been during every practice session in their small kitchen. Lucía lifted her bow, and the first note came out clear and steady.

**According to the text, what helps Lucía begin playing with confidence?**

- A) She realizes that the audience is smaller than she expected.
- B) She remembers advice that her violin teacher once gave her.
- C) She sees her grandmother sitting in the same way she did during their practice sessions.
- D) She notices that her hands are no longer tired from months of practice.

**Correct answer: C**

**Key explanation.** At first Lucía feels nervous (“her hands felt as if they belonged to someone else”). Then she sees her grandmother in the same posture as “during every practice session in their small kitchen.” Immediately after, her first note is “clear and steady.” The familiar sight connects the stressful stage to comfortable practice at home.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** The text never mentions the size of the audience.
- **B:** No violin teacher appears in the text.
- **D:** Lucía’s hands felt strange, not tired, and the text does not say the feeling simply disappeared on its own.

**Tip.** “According to the text” means the answer is stated or directly shown in the passage. Find the sentence just before Lucía’s playing changes.

### Question 10 [HARD]

**Information and Ideas / Command of Evidence (Textual)**. ⏱ Target: 90 seconds

> Agricultural scientist Kwame Mensah hypothesizes that intercropping, which means growing two crops together in the same field, can reduce pest damage because the second crop confuses insects that find their preferred plants by smell. To test this idea, Mensah’s team planted maize alone in some fields and maize together with a strong-smelling herb in others.

**Which finding, if true, would most directly support Mensah’s hypothesis?**

- A) Maize plants grown with the herb grew slightly taller than maize plants grown alone.
- B) Insects that locate maize by smell were found far less often on maize grown with the herb than on maize grown alone, while insects that locate maize by sight were found equally often in both types of fields.
- C) In fields where the two crops were grown together, the herb suffered more insect damage than the maize did.
- D) Farmers in the region said they preferred to grow maize alone because intercropping required more labor.

**Correct answer: B**

**Key explanation.** The hypothesis has two parts: intercropping reduces pests, and it does so by confusing insects that use smell. Choice B supports both parts. Smell-guided insects decreased when the herb was present, while sight-guided insects, which the herb’s smell should not affect, did not change. This pattern points directly to smell as the cause.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** Plant height is not related to pest damage or to how insects find plants.
- **C:** This shows the herb was attacked, but it says nothing about whether damage to maize fell or whether smell was involved.
- **D:** Farmers’ preferences about labor do not test whether intercropping confuses insects.

**Tip.** For “support the hypothesis” questions, identify the mechanism (the “because” part). The best evidence supports the mechanism and rules out other explanations.

### Question 11 [MEDIUM]

**Information and Ideas / Command of Evidence (Quantitative)**. ⏱ Target: 75 seconds

**Approximate Share of Electricity Generated from Three Renewable Sources, 2022 (%)**

| Country | Hydropower | Wind | Geothermal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Norway | 88 | 10 | 0 |
| Kenya | 21 | 17 | 45 |
| Denmark | 0 | 55 | 0 |
| Iceland | 70 | 0 | 30 |

*Values are rounded.*


> Kenya lies along the East African Rift, a region where heat from deep inside the Earth rises close to the surface. Kenyan engineers have used this heat to build large geothermal power plants, including the Olkaria complex in the Rift Valley. As a result, geothermal energy has become Kenya’s leading source of electricity among the sources shown in the table: ______

**Which choice most effectively uses data from the table to complete the statement?**

- A) Kenya generates about 45% of its electricity from geothermal energy, compared with about 21% from hydropower and about 17% from wind.
- B) Kenya generates about 45% of its electricity from geothermal energy, while Iceland generates about 30% of its electricity from geothermal energy.
- C) Kenya generates about 21% of its electricity from hydropower, while Norway generates about 88% of its electricity from hydropower.
- D) Kenya generates about 17% of its electricity from wind, compared with about 55% in Denmark.

**Correct answer: A**

**Key explanation.** The claim is that geothermal energy is Kenya’s leading source among the three sources in the table. To support it, the data must compare Kenya’s geothermal share with Kenya’s other sources. Choice A does this: 45% geothermal is greater than 21% hydropower and 17% wind.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **B:** This is accurate, but comparing Kenya with Iceland does not show that geothermal energy is Kenya’s leading source.
- **C:** This is accurate, but it focuses on hydropower and compares Kenya with Norway. It says nothing about geothermal energy.
- **D:** This is accurate, but it compares wind power in Kenya and Denmark, which is irrelevant to the claim.

**Tip.** Data questions often include choices that are true according to the table but do not support the claim. Check two things: Is the choice accurate? Does it prove this specific claim?

### Question 12 [HARD]

**Information and Ideas / Command of Evidence (Quantitative)**. ⏱ Target: 105 seconds

**Grain Yield of Three Rice Varieties Under Normal and Flooded Conditions** (bar graph; Yield (tonnes per hectare))

|  | Normal conditions | Flooded for 10 days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Variety P | 5.8 | 2.1 |
| Variety Q | 5.1 | 4.6 |
| Variety R | 6.2 | 3 |


> Rice farmers in the Mekong Delta of Vietnam face increasingly frequent floods. A team of agronomists grew three rice varieties (P, Q, and R) under normal conditions and under conditions in which fields were flooded for 10 days. The team concluded that although variety Q is not the highest-yielding variety under normal conditions, it would be the best choice for farmers in areas likely to flood.

**Which choice best describes data from the graph that support the team’s conclusion?**

- A) Under normal conditions, variety R produced a higher yield than either variety P or variety Q.
- B) Variety Q’s yield decreased by less than 1 tonne per hectare when fields were flooded, and under flooded conditions variety Q produced a higher yield than either variety P or variety R.
- C) All three varieties produced lower yields under flooded conditions than under normal conditions.
- D) Under flooded conditions, variety P produced less than half of its yield under normal conditions.

**Correct answer: B**

**Key explanation.** The conclusion has two parts: Q is not the best variety under normal conditions, but it is the best choice where floods are likely. Choice B supports the second, key part. Q lost only 0.5 tonnes per hectare (5.1 → 4.6), and its flooded yield (4.6) is higher than P’s (2.1) and R’s (3.0).

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** This is true (6.2 is the highest normal yield), but it only supports the minor point that Q is not the top variety under normal conditions. It says nothing about flooding.
- **C:** This is true, but it does not distinguish Q from the other varieties, so it cannot show that Q is the best choice.
- **D:** This is true (2.1 is less than half of 5.8), but it describes only variety P and does not show that Q is best.

**Tip.** When a conclusion contains “although,” the part after the comma is usually the main claim. Choose the data that support the main claim.

### Question 13 [MEDIUM]

**Information and Ideas / Inferences**. ⏱ Target: 75 seconds

> Archaeologists studying Mohenjo-daro, an ancient city in present-day Pakistan that was built more than 4,000 years ago, have found that many houses were connected to covered drains running beneath the streets. For the drains to work, they had to slope gently in the correct direction across large parts of the city, which would have been difficult to achieve if each street had been designed separately. These findings suggest that ______

**Which choice most logically completes the text?**

- A) the residents of Mohenjo-daro built their homes one at a time, without any shared plan.
- B) the drainage system of Mohenjo-daro was later copied by cities in other parts of the world.
- C) the construction of Mohenjo-daro was likely guided by some form of coordinated planning.
- D) the drains were added to Mohenjo-daro long after most of its houses had been built.

**Correct answer: C**

**Key explanation.** The text says the drains had to slope correctly across large parts of the city and that this would have been difficult if each street had been designed separately. The logical conclusion is that the city was built according to a coordinated, city-wide plan.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** This contradicts the text’s reasoning: without a shared plan, a city-wide sloping drain system would have been difficult to build.
- **B:** The text gives no information about other cities or about copying.
- **D:** The text does not discuss when the drains were built compared with the houses, so nothing supports this timeline.

### Question 14 [HARD]

**Information and Ideas / Inferences**. ⏱ Target: 105 seconds

> Honeybees communicate the location of food through a “waggle dance,” in which the angle of the dance shows the direction of the food relative to the Sun. Biologist Hana Sato noticed that bees in her colony still danced accurately on cloudy days when the Sun itself was hidden. Because bees can detect patterns of polarized light, which remain visible in patches of blue sky even when the Sun is covered, Sato hypothesized that the bees were using these light patterns to locate the Sun. If Sato’s hypothesis is correct, then on days when thick clouds cover the entire sky and no blue patches are visible, bees would most likely ______

**Which choice most logically completes the text?**

- A) dance more accurately than they do on clear days.
- B) stop searching for food for the rest of the season.
- C) dance less accurately than they do on cloudy days with patches of blue sky.
- D) perform the waggle dance at the same angle regardless of where the food is located.

**Correct answer: C**

**Key explanation.** According to the hypothesis, bees use polarized-light patterns in patches of blue sky to locate the Sun. If no blue patches are visible, the bees lose this source of information, so their dances should become less accurate than on days when some blue sky remains.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** Losing directional information would not make dances more accurate than on clear days, when the Sun is fully visible.
- **B:** Nothing in the text suggests bees stop searching for food; this is far more extreme than the hypothesis supports.
- **D:** This extreme claim (that the dance would stop showing direction entirely) goes beyond the text. The hypothesis supports reduced accuracy, not a complete loss of meaning.

**Tip.** “If the hypothesis is correct” questions ask you to apply the hypothesis to a new situation. Remove the key factor (here, blue sky) and predict what changes.

### Question 15 [EASY]

**Standard English Conventions / Boundaries**. ⏱ Target: 40 seconds

> The Atacama Desert in northern Chile is one of the driest places on Earth; some weather stations there have recorded almost no rain for decades. Because the air above the desert is so dry and ______ astronomers from many countries have built telescopes in the region.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

- A) clear,
- B) clear;
- C) clear.
- D) clear:

**Correct answer: A**

**Key explanation.** The sentence begins with a dependent clause (“Because the air above the desert is so dry and clear”). A comma is the standard way to separate an introductory dependent clause from the main clause (“astronomers … have built telescopes”).

**Distractor explanations.**

- **B:** A semicolon must join two independent clauses. “Because the air … clear” cannot stand alone as a sentence.
- **C:** A period would leave “Because the air above the desert is so dry and clear” as a sentence fragment.
- **D:** A colon must follow an independent clause. The clause before it begins with “Because,” so it is dependent.

**Tip.** Words such as “because,” “although,” “when,” and “if” make a clause dependent. A dependent clause cannot come before a period, semicolon, or colon.

### Question 16 [EASY]

**Standard English Conventions / Form, Structure, and Sense**. ⏱ Target: 40 seconds

> In 1953, Tenzing Norgay and Edmund Hillary ______ the first climbers confirmed to have reached the summit of Mount Everest, which rises 8,849 meters above sea level.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

- A) have become
- B) will become
- C) become
- D) became

**Correct answer: D**

**Key explanation.** The phrase “In 1953” shows that the action was completed at a specific time in the past. The simple past tense “became” is correct.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** The present perfect (“have become”) cannot be used with a specific finished time such as “In 1953.”
- **B:** The future tense does not fit an event that happened in 1953.
- **C:** The present tense does not fit an event that happened in 1953.

**Tip.** Common error for international students: in languages such as French and German, the form that looks like the English present perfect is often used with past dates. In English, a specific past date (“In 1953,” “last year”) requires the simple past.

### Question 17 [MEDIUM]

**Standard English Conventions / Boundaries**. ⏱ Target: 45 seconds

> In 2004, Kenyan environmental activist Wangari Maathai became the first African woman to receive the Nobel Peace ______ was honored for founding the Green Belt Movement, which has planted tens of millions of trees across Kenya.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

- A) Prize, she
- B) Prize; she
- C) Prize she
- D) Prize and, she

**Correct answer: B**

**Key explanation.** The text contains two independent clauses: “Wangari Maathai became the first African woman to receive the Nobel Peace Prize” and “she was honored for founding the Green Belt Movement.” A semicolon correctly joins two independent clauses without a conjunction.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** A comma alone cannot join two independent clauses; this creates a comma splice.
- **C:** With no punctuation, the two independent clauses run together, creating a run-on sentence.
- **D:** The comma is in the wrong place. When “and” joins two independent clauses, the comma goes before “and,” not after it.

**Tip.** Some languages, such as Chinese, regularly join complete sentences with commas. Formal English never does. Two complete sentences need a period, a semicolon, or a comma plus a joining word (and, but, so).

### Question 18 [MEDIUM]

**Standard English Conventions / Boundaries**. ⏱ Target: 50 seconds

> Hangul, the writing system used for the Korean ______ was created in the 1440s under the direction of King Sejong, who wanted ordinary people to be able to read and write with ease.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

- A) language;
- B) language—
- C) language,
- D) language

**Correct answer: C**

**Key explanation.** The phrase “the writing system used for the Korean language” is extra information (an appositive) that describes Hangul. It begins with a comma after “Hangul,” so it must also end with a comma.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** A semicolon cannot end an appositive; semicolons separate independent clauses or complex list items.
- **B:** An appositive must begin and end with the same punctuation mark. Because it begins with a comma, it cannot end with a dash.
- **D:** Without a closing comma, the appositive is not separated from the rest of the sentence, and the subject “Hangul” is cut off from its verb “was created.”

**Tip.** Test it by removing the extra information: “Hangul … was created in the 1440s” must still be a complete, correct sentence.

### Question 19 [MEDIUM]

**Standard English Conventions / Form, Structure, and Sense**. ⏱ Target: 50 seconds

> The collection of handwoven carpets on display at the museum in Isfahan, Iran, ______ designs that date back more than four hundred years.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

- A) include
- B) have included
- C) are including
- D) includes

**Correct answer: D**

**Key explanation.** The subject of the sentence is “collection,” which is singular. The phrase “of handwoven carpets” describes the collection but does not change the subject. A singular subject requires the singular verb “includes.”

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** “Include” is a plural verb. It wrongly agrees with “carpets,” which is part of a prepositional phrase, not the subject.
- **B:** “Have included” is plural and does not agree with the singular subject “collection.”
- **C:** “Are including” is plural and does not agree with the singular subject “collection.”

**Tip.** Cross out prepositional phrases (of …, at …, in …) to find the true subject. Many test takers make the verb agree with the nearest noun (“carpets”) instead of the subject.

### Question 20 [HARD]

**Standard English Conventions / Boundaries**. ⏱ Target: 60 seconds

> The international research team studying urban heat collected temperature data in three rapidly growing cities: Lagos, ______ Hanoi, Vietnam; and Lima, Peru.

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

- A) Nigeria:
- B) Nigeria,
- C) Nigeria
- D) Nigeria;

**Correct answer: D**

**Key explanation.** The items in this list contain their own commas (“Lagos, Nigeria”). To avoid confusion, such items must be separated by semicolons, as the text already does between “Vietnam” and “and Lima.” A semicolon is therefore needed after “Nigeria.”

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** A colon introduces a list or an explanation. It cannot separate items inside a list that has already been introduced.
- **B:** A comma would make it unclear where each item ends (“Lagos,” “Nigeria,” and “Hanoi” could look like three separate items). It also breaks the pattern of semicolons used later in the list.
- **C:** Without punctuation, “Nigeria Hanoi” runs together, and the list items are not separated.

**Tip.** Look for a pattern. The text already uses “; and” before the final item, so the same punctuation must separate every item in the list.

### Question 21 [HARD]

**Standard English Conventions / Form, Structure, and Sense**. ⏱ Target: 60 seconds

> Having analyzed thousands of satellite images of the Amazon rainforest, ______

**Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?**

- A) the scientists’ finding was that the rate of deforestation had slowed in some regions.
- B) some regions showed a slower rate of deforestation, according to the scientists.
- C) the scientists found that the rate of deforestation had slowed in some regions.
- D) the rate of deforestation was found by the scientists to have slowed in some regions.

**Correct answer: C**

**Key explanation.** The opening phrase “Having analyzed thousands of satellite images” describes whoever did the analyzing. The noun immediately after the comma must be that person or group. Only choice C places “the scientists” right after the phrase.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** This says that the “finding” analyzed the images. A finding cannot analyze anything, so the modifier is misplaced.
- **B:** This says that “some regions” analyzed the images, which is illogical.
- **D:** This says that “the rate of deforestation” analyzed the images, which is illogical.

**Tip.** Ask who did the action in the opening phrase. That person or thing must come immediately after the comma.

### Question 22 [EASY]

**Expression of Ideas / Transitions**. ⏱ Target: 45 seconds

> Traditional Japanese houses often use sliding paper screens called *shoji* to divide rooms. Because the screens are lightweight, they can be moved easily to change the size and shape of a room. ______ a single space can serve as a dining area during the day and a bedroom at night.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?**

- A) In contrast,
- B) Similarly,
- C) Even so,
- D) For this reason,

**Correct answer: D**

**Key explanation.** The previous sentence explains that shoji screens can be moved easily to change a room’s size and shape. The final sentence gives a result of that flexibility: one space can have different uses. “For this reason” correctly signals cause and effect.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** “In contrast” signals a difference, but the final sentence continues the idea of flexibility rather than opposing it.
- **B:** “Similarly” introduces a separate, comparable example. The final sentence is a consequence of the previous one, not a similar case.
- **C:** “Even so” signals that something happens despite the previous information. The flexible use of space happens because of the movable screens, not in spite of them.

### Question 23 [MEDIUM]

**Expression of Ideas / Transitions**. ⏱ Target: 50 seconds

> For many years, scientists believed that the deep ocean floor contained very little life, because sunlight cannot reach depths below about 1,000 meters and most food chains depend on sunlight. ______ in 1977, researchers exploring the sea floor near the Galápagos Islands discovered thriving communities of giant tubeworms, clams, and bacteria that obtain energy from chemicals rather than from sunlight.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?**

- A) However,
- B) Similarly,
- C) Therefore,
- D) For example,

**Correct answer: A**

**Key explanation.** The first sentence describes an old belief: the deep ocean floor had little life. The second describes a discovery that contradicts this belief: thriving communities of living things. “However” correctly signals this contrast.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **B:** “Similarly” signals agreement between two ideas, but the discovery challenges the earlier belief.
- **C:** “Therefore” signals a result. The discovery was not caused by the belief; it disproved the belief.
- **D:** “For example” introduces an illustration of the previous idea. The discovery is not an example of a lifeless ocean floor; it is evidence against that idea.

### Question 24 [HARD]

**Expression of Ideas / Transitions**. ⏱ Target: 70 seconds

> Coffee originated in the highlands of Ethiopia, where wild coffee plants still grow in shaded forests. These wild plants contain far more genetic variety than the few types grown on large farms around the world, so scientists consider Ethiopia’s forests a valuable resource. ______ if a new disease were to threaten cultivated coffee, researchers might find disease-resistant genes among the wild plants and use them to breed stronger varieties.

**Which choice completes the text with the most logical transition?**

- A) Nonetheless,
- B) Specifically,
- C) Alternatively,
- D) Meanwhile,

**Correct answer: B**

**Key explanation.** The previous sentence makes a general claim: Ethiopia’s wild coffee forests are “a valuable resource.” The final sentence explains exactly how they are valuable: they could provide genes that protect cultivated coffee from disease. “Specifically” correctly introduces a more precise explanation of a general statement.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** “Nonetheless” signals a contrast (“despite this”). The final sentence supports the claim that the forests are valuable; it does not contrast with it.
- **C:** “Alternatively” introduces a different option. The final sentence is not an alternative; it explains why the forests are valuable.
- **D:** “Meanwhile” signals that two things happen at the same time. The final sentence is not about timing; it develops the previous idea.

### Question 25 [MEDIUM]

**Expression of Ideas / Rhetorical Synthesis**. ⏱ Target: 60 seconds

> While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
> 
> - The Silk Road was a network of trade routes connecting East Asia with the Mediterranean region.
> - It was used from around the second century BCE to the fifteenth century CE.
> - Merchants traded goods such as silk, spices, and paper.
> - Ideas, religions, and technologies also spread along the routes.
> - Cities such as Samarkand, in present-day Uzbekistan, grew wealthy from the trade.

**The student wants to emphasize that the Silk Road spread more than physical goods. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?**

- A) The Silk Road, a network of trade routes, was used from around the second century BCE to the fifteenth century CE.
- B) Samarkand, a city in present-day Uzbekistan, grew wealthy from trade along the Silk Road.
- C) Merchants on the Silk Road traded goods such as silk, spices, and paper between East Asia and the Mediterranean region.
- D) Along the Silk Road, merchants carried not only silk, spices, and paper but also ideas, religions, and technologies.

**Correct answer: D**

**Key explanation.** The goal is to show that the Silk Road spread more than physical goods. Choice D uses the structure “not only … but also” to connect physical goods (silk, spices, paper) with things that are not physical (ideas, religions, technologies).

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** This gives the dates when the Silk Road was used but does not mention what spread along it.
- **B:** This describes the wealth of one city and does not mention anything that spread besides trade goods.

### Question 26 [MEDIUM]

> - Tu Youyou is a Chinese pharmaceutical chemist.

### Question 27 [MEDIUM]

**Expression of Ideas / Rhetorical Synthesis**. ⏱ Target: 70 seconds

> While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:
> 
> - The Sahara and the Gobi are two of the world’s largest deserts.
> - The Sahara, in northern Africa, covers about 9.2 million square kilometers.
> - The Gobi, in northern China and southern Mongolia, covers about 1.3 million square kilometers.
> - Summer temperatures in the Sahara often rise above 40°C.
> - Winter temperatures in the Gobi can fall below −40°C.
> - The Sahara is classified as a hot desert, while the Gobi is classified as a cold desert.

**The student wants to emphasize a difference in temperature between the two deserts. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?**

- A) The Sahara covers about 9.2 million square kilometers, while the Gobi covers about 1.3 million square kilometers.
- B) The Sahara is a hot desert where summer temperatures often rise above 40°C, whereas the Gobi is a cold desert where winter temperatures can fall below −40°C.
- C) The Gobi, which is located in northern China and southern Mongolia, is one of the world’s largest deserts.
- D) The Sahara and the Gobi, two of the world’s largest deserts, are located in northern Africa and in Asia, respectively.

**Correct answer: B**

**Key explanation.** Choice B contrasts the deserts’ temperatures using “whereas” and gives specific data for each: above 40°C in the Sahara’s summer versus below −40°C in the Gobi’s winter.

**Distractor explanations.**

- **A:** This emphasizes a difference in size, not temperature.
- **C:** This gives the Gobi’s location and does not mention temperature or the Sahara.

**Tip.** Contrast words such as “whereas,” “while,” and “although” are strong signals when a goal asks you to emphasize a difference.
